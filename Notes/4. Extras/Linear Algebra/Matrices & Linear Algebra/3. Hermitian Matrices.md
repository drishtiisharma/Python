# Hermitian Matrices

 A square matrix is said to be a [Hermitian matrix](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/uploads/20250812184222360723/hermitian-matrix.webp) if it is equal to its conjugate transpose matrix.
 It is a square matrix that has complex numbers except for the diagonal entries, which are real numbers.

**Example**

Consider the following complex square matrix $A$:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 
8 & 1-i \\ 
1+i & 5 
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 1: Compute the Complex Conjugate $\bar{A}$**
To find the conjugate, we change the sign of the imaginary part for each element ($i \to -i$):

$$
\bar{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 
8 & 1+i \\ 
1-i & 5 
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 2: Compute the Conjugate Transpose $A^{\dagger}$ (or $A^H$)**
To find the conjugate transpose, we take the transpose of $\bar{A}$ (swap rows and columns):

$$
A^{\dagger} = (\bar{A})^T = \begin{bmatrix} 
8 & 1-i \\ 
1+i & 5 
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Conclusion**
By comparing the original matrix $A$ and the conjugate transpose $A^{\dagger}$:

$$
A = A^{\dagger}
$$

Since the matrix is equal to its own conjugate transpose, **$A$ is a Hermitian matrix**.


# Skew-Hermitian Matrices

A complex square matrix is said to be a [Skew- Hermitian matrix](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/uploads/20250812185419020313/skew-hermitian.webp) if the conjugate transpose matrix is equal to the negative of the original matrix.
