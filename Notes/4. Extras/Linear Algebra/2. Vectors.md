## **Vectors**

A vector is an ordered list of numbers representing both magnitude and direction.

For example:

A 3-dimensional vector consists of three components (often labeled x, y, and z) and can be written as:

$$ \vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} v_x \\ v_y \\ v_z \end{bmatrix} $$

It represents a quantity with both **magnitude** (length) and **direction** in 3D space. Common examples include position, velocity, and force vectors in physics and computer graphics.

## Vector as a point

A vector can also represent a point in space. In this case, the 2D vector:

$$ \vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

represents the point **(3, 2)** in Cartesian coordinates.

This means:
- **3 units** along the x-axis (horizontal)
- **2 units** along the y-axis (vertical)

The vector essentially tells us how to get from the origin (0, 0) to that point by moving 3 units right and 2 units up.
