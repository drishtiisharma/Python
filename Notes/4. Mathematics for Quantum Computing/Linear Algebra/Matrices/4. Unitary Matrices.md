# Unitary Matrices

A unitary matrix is a square complex matrix where the inverse of the matrix is equal to its conjugate transpose. This property preserves the length (norm) of vectors and the angles between them during transformation, making it fundamental in quantum mechanics and signal processing.

### Definition
A square matrix $U$ of size $n \times n$ is unitary if:

$$
U^{\dagger} U = U U^{\dagger} = I_n
$$

Where:
- $U^{\dagger}$ denotes the **conjugate transpose** (also called Hermitian adjoint). To compute this, you first take the transpose of $U$, then take the complex conjugate of every entry.
- $I_n$ is the $n \times n$ identity matrix.
- All entries can be complex numbers. If all entries are real, a unitary matrix is simply an **orthogonal matrix**.

### Key Mathematical Properties
1. **Inverse:** $U^{-1} = U^{\dagger}$
2. **Determinant:** The absolute value of the determinant is always 1: $|\det(U)| = 1$
3. **Eigenvalues:** All eigenvalues $\lambda$ lie on the unit circle in the complex plane: $|\lambda| = 1$
4. **Norm Preservation:** For any vector $x$, $\|Ux\| = \|x\|$
5. **Orthonormal Columns/Rows:** The columns (and rows) form an orthonormal set with respect to the standard complex inner product $\langle u, v \rangle = v^{\dagger} u$
## Example

Consider the following $2 \times 2$ complex matrix:

$$
U = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

We will verify whether $U$ is unitary by checking if $U^{\dagger} U = I$.

**Step 1: Compute the Conjugate Transpose $U^{\dagger}$**

First, transpose $U$:

$$
U^{T} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Next, take the complex conjugate of each element (replace $i$ with $-i$):

$$
U^{\dagger} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 2: Multiply $U^{\dagger} U$**

$$
U^{\dagger} U = \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix} \right) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix} \right)
$$

Factor out the scalars: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2}$

Now perform matrix multiplication:

$$
= \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} (1)(1) + (-i)(i) & (1)(i) + (-i)(1) \\ (-i)(1) + (1)(i) & (-i)(i) + (1)(1) \end{bmatrix}
$$

Compute each entry:

- **Row 1, Col 1:** $1 + (-i)(i) = 1 - i^2 = 1 - (-1) = 2$
- **Row 1, Col 2:** $i + (-i) = 0$
- **Row 2, Col 1:** $-i + i = 0$
- **Row 2, Col 2:** $(-i)(i) + 1 = -i^2 + 1 = 1 + 1 = 2$

$$
= \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = I_2
$$

**Conclusion**
Since $U^{\dagger} U = I_2$, the matrix $U$ is confirmed to be unitary.

### Quick Verification of Determinant Property

$$
\det(U) = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 \left[ (1)(1) - (i)(i) \right] = \frac{1}{2}(1 - i^2) = \frac{1}{2}(1 + 1) = 1
$$

$$
|\det(U)| = |1| = 1 \quad \checkmark
$$

This confirms the theoretical property that the magnitude of the determinant of any unitary matrix equals 1.
