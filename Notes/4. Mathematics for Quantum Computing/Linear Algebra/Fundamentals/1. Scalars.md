# Scalars

A **scalar** is simply a single number that represents only **magnitude** (size or amount). It has **no direction**.
## Key Characteristics:
1.  **Single Value:** It’s just one number (integer, float, etc.).
2.  **No Direction:** It doesn’t point anywhere in space.
3.  **Scaling:** In linear algebra, scalars are used to "scale" vectors (make them longer or shorter).
## Examples of Scalars:
*   **Temperature:** 25°C (It’s just a value; it doesn’t point north or south.)
*   **Mass:** 70 kg (Your weight doesn’t have a direction.)
*   **Time:** 10 seconds
*   **Price:** $5.99

### Example of Scaling a Vector with a Scalar:
Imagine you have a vector representing a movement of 2 steps right and 1 step up:
$$ \vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

If you multiply this by the **scalar** `3`:
$$ 3 \times \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} $$

The result is a new vector that is **3 times larger** in magnitude but points in the **same direction**. The scalar `3` simply scaled the original vector.

## Comparison:
| Type | Example | Has Direction? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scalar** | Speed: 50 mph | ❌ No |
| **Vector** | Velocity: 50 mph **North** | ✅ Yes |
